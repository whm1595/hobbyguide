Leatherworking QuickStart

Make a card holder on your kitchen table this weekend. No shop, no sewing machine, no power tools — just sharp edges, waxed thread, and two needles.

⏱️ 2–3 hrs first project 💸 $60–$120 to begin 🪡 Kitchen-table friendly 🗓️ Updated June 2026

Start with vegetable-tanned leather

There are two main types of leather you'll see: veg-tan (vegetable-tanned) and chrome-tan. Veg-tan is the stiff, pale, honey-colored leather used for belts and wallets — it holds shape, stamps beautifully, and reveals every mistake (which sounds bad, but is actually how you learn). Chrome-tan is softer and better for bags and clothing, but it's harder to work with as a beginner. Start with 4–6 oz veg-tan. A quarter-hide or a 12"×12" square is plenty for a dozen practice projects.

The one technique that matters: the saddle stitch

Leatherworkers don't sew the way garment-makers do. The saddle stitch uses two needles on one piece of waxed thread, passing through the same holes from opposite sides. It's stronger than anything a sewing machine can do — if one stitch breaks, the rest don't unravel. Everything else in leatherwork is cutting, punching, and edge-finishing. If you nail the saddle stitch, you can make almost anything.

Your first project: a two-pocket card holder

Small, useful, and it teaches every fundamental skill. Three rectangles of leather, three edges of saddle stitching.

  1. Cut three rectangles. Two at 3" × 4" (the pockets), one at 3" × 4" (the back). Use a straightedge and a fresh blade; cut in one firm pass, not a sawing motion.
  2. Bevel and burnish the top edges of the pockets. These will stay exposed on the finished piece, so finish them before you assemble. Beveler to chamfer, then burnish with water and a canvas scrap until the edge is smooth and glossy.
  3. Glue the stack temporarily. A thin line of contact cement or even double-sided tape along the bottom and sides — just enough to keep the layers from shifting while you punch holes.
  4. Mark and punch your stitching line. Draw a line 4mm (~⅛") in from the edge with a pencil. Walk your chisel along it, punching through all layers.
  5. Saddle stitch. Cut thread ~4× the length of your seam. Thread a needle onto each end. Pass one needle through the first hole, pull until the thread is even. Then: left needle through next hole, right needle through the same hole, pull both tight. Repeat. It's meditative once you find the rhythm.
  6. Backstitch the last two holes. Reverse direction through the last two holes to lock the seam. Trim threads close and burn the ends with a lighter to fuse them.
  7. Bevel and burnish the assembled edges. The edges you just stitched are now exposed. Bevel both sides, burnish with water until glossy.
Practice the saddle stitch on scrap first. Make a 3" × 1" strip of scrap leather. Punch a line of holes. Stitch it end-to-end. Rip it out. Do it again. Twenty minutes of this on scrap saves you from doing it on your actual project with shaking hands.

Where to go next

  • Your second project: a simple belt. It teaches straight cuts, edge finishing at scale, and how to set snaps.
  • Your third: a bifold wallet — multiple pockets, thinner leather, more precise stitching. The classic "am I actually a leatherworker yet" test.
  • r/Leathercraft for patterns, critique, and suppliers. Unusually welcoming community.
  • YouTube: Ian Atkinson, Corter Leather, and Little King Goods all make exceptional beginner-to-intermediate tutorials.
  • Once you're confident: try skiving (thinning leather at seams so assembled layers don't bulge) and dyeing (Fiebing's is the standard brand).

Beginner FAQ

What is the difference between 'genuine leather' and the good stuff?

Counterintuitively, 'genuine leather' is a low grade — often bonded scraps. Look for 'full-grain' or 'veg-tan' instead. For learning, 4–6 oz vegetable-tanned leather is the standard.

Why do my stitches look crooked?

Usually inconsistent chisel placement or switching which needle goes through the hole first. Keep the same needle order every stitch, and practice a few rows on scrap before every project.

Is leatherworking expensive?

Starting costs $60–120 for tools that last years, and practice leather is cheap — many suppliers sell scrap bundles by the pound. It is one of the cheaper craft hobbies to maintain.
